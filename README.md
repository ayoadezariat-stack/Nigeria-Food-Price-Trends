Nigeria Food Price Trends

An Analysis of Essential Food Prices Between January 2025 and January 2026

Tools: Microsoft Excel | Power Query | Power BI | DAX

Project Overview
This project examines the prices of 42 selected food items in Nigeria between January 2025 and January 2026. It looks at year-on-year and month-on-month price changes, differences across the six geopolitical zones, and food items with large regional price differences.

The aim is to understand how these price patterns could inform purchasing, pricing and distribution decisions for a food distribution business.

Business Problem
Food prices can vary over time, across products and between regions. A food distribution business needs to understand these differences when deciding what to purchase, where to source products and how to plan its pricing.

This project uses reported food price data to examine these patterns and identify areas that may require closer attention.

Dataset
Source: Nigeria National Bureau of Statistics (NBS), Selected Food Price Watch, January 2026.

The analysis covers 42 selected food items across:
* North Central
* North East
* North West
* South East
* South South
* South West

The periods examined are January 2025, December 2025 and January 2026.

Month-on-Month Changes
The largest increases between December 2025 and January 2026 included Chicken Wings (3.72%), Groundnut Oil 75cl (1.70%) and Tilapia Fish (1.55%).

The largest decreases included Beef Boneless (-4.19%), Goat Meat bone-in (-3.07%) and Vegetable Oil 75cl (-2.44%).

Business Recommendations
* Monitor products with large year-on-year increases when planning purchases and pricing.
* Compare prices across regions before making sourcing decisions.
* Consider lower reported prices as potential sourcing opportunities, while accounting for transport, availability and other costs.
* Review products with substantial price declines before making purchasing or pricing adjustments.
* Continue monitoring prices monthly to identify changes early.

Limitations
This analysis covers 42 selected food items, not the entire Nigerian food market. The average is calculated across reported prices for items that may have different units or characteristics. It is not a weighted consumer price index and should not be interpreted as an official inflation measure.

Regional price differences also do not account for transport costs, availability, product quality or other operating expenses.

Conclusion
The selected food items showed different price movements over the period, and reported prices varied across geopolitical zones. These differences can be useful when reviewing purchasing and distribution decisions, provided other business costs are also considered.
